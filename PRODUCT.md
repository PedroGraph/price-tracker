# Product

## Register

product

## Users

One person tracking Amazon prices from a desktop app that lives in the tray. They add products from the cart, wishlists, saved-for-later or a pasted link, and check the dashboard a few times a day to decide whether to buy now or wait. Purchases are usually shipped to Colombia, so the dollar rate, shipping and import fees matter as much as the sticker price.

## Product Purpose

Watch the products the user cares about, record every price, and surface the moments worth acting on: a real drop, an all-time low, a target hit, low stock, or a cheaper price inside the return window. Alerts reach the user by email, Telegram, desktop notification and webhooks; the app also answers "what can I buy with this budget" and "is this a good price". Success is the user buying at the right moment with confidence, not checking Amazon by hand.

## Brand Personality

Calm, precise, quietly premium. The interface should feel like a well-made native tool: dark glass surfaces, one teal accent, tabular numbers, motion that only confirms state. Spanish-first voice, direct and specific, no marketing tone.

## Anti-references

Coupon-clipper clutter, red "SALE" badges everywhere, dense e-commerce listing grids, neon dashboards, gradient-heavy fintech chrome, decorative illustration. Nothing that makes the app look like an affiliate store page.

## Design Principles

1. The price is the answer. Every surface leads with the number the user came for; everything else supports it.
2. One glance per product. A card must be scannable in under a second: what it is, what it costs, how it moved.
3. Neutral surfaces, color for state. Teal means better/active, amber means caution, red means loss; never decoration.
4. Density with air. Many products on screen, but each block keeps clear hierarchy and breathing room.
5. Motion confirms, never performs. Hover, press and reveal only.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA contrast for body and placeholder text in both themes, visible keyboard focus on every control, reduced-motion alternative for all transitions, and state never carried by color alone (always paired with a label or icon).
